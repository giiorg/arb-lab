A · Instrument

Near-black, monospace throughout, hairline rules, no cards and no shadows. Colour is reserved — it only ever means state, never decoration. Descended from a trading terminal but properly typeset. Densest of the three: more rows per screen than either alternative, and nothing on screen that isn't a number or its label.

B · Editorial

Light-first, warm paper ground, serif display figures against a sans UI. Generous spacing, very restrained colour. Reads like a research publication — which is what this project currently is. Least dense; best for reading evidence, worst for scanning thirty live rows.

C · Console

Dark, elevated surfaces, rounded corners, pill badges, sans labels over monospace numbers. The well-made-SaaS register — immediately legible to anyone, most familiar on a phone. Costs vertical space to chrome, and the pills compete with the numbers for attention.

My recommendation: A, warmed slightly. Keep A's discipline — hairlines instead of cards, monospace numerics, colour that only ever means state — but lift the background off pure black, widen the row rhythm a touch, and borrow B's typographic care for headings. That gives you density where it matters (a signal board over 61 symbols) without the harshness. C is the safe choice and would look fine, but its chrome buys nothing on a screen whose entire job is numbers, and rounded pills next to a red −0.0421% dilute the one signal you actually need to see.
