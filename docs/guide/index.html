    Spot, futures, perpetuals

    Three instruments, and the differences between them are the whole reason this trade exists.

    Spot You buy the asset and own it. No leverage, no funding, and nobody force-closes it.
    Futures An agreement to trade at a future date and price. It expires, and at expiry its price must meet spot.
    Perpetual A future with no expiry. Nothing forces it back to spot, so something else has to.

    That last row is the interesting one. A future converges on spot because it has to settle. A perpetual never settles, so the exchange needs a different tether — and that tether is the funding rate. tutorial §1.1

    One caveat about "nobody force-closes your spot"

    Holding 1 BTC outright means no liquidation engine can take it from you. But whether that spot can be pulled in to cover losses on your derivatives position is a different question, and the answer depends on how the account is configured. This project deliberately pins a posture where it cannot — segregated, isolated, verified at boot and before every trade — because the spot leg being untouchable is the premise the entire hedge rests on. core §4.1

    If that premise is wrong, a bad enough move on the perp leg starts eating the spot leg that was supposed to be hedging it, and the position is not what you think it is. Which is why posture is verified rather than assumed. tutorial §9

    The funding rate

    A perpetual can drift away from spot. Nothing forces them back together, because there is no settlement date to converge on. So the exchange makes the crowded side pay the other side, at fixed intervals. tutorial §1.2

    • Perp trading above spot — longs are crowded — funding is positive, and longs pay shorts.
    • Perp trading below spot — funding is negative, and shorts pay longs.

    The exchange orchestrates the transfer and takes no side. Funding is counterparties paying each other to hold the tether. Being short the perp while long the spot is how you get paid for it. tutorial §1.2

    The damper is not the cap

    Simplified, the rate is F ≈ premium_index + clamp(interest − premium, ±damper). Two different clamps live in that sentence and they get conflated constantly. tutorial §1.4

    The damper (Binance ~±0.05%) A small band inside the formula. Keeps the rate near the interest term when the premium is small. Shapes normal rates.
    The cap / floor (per-symbol; BTC ±0.3%, alts ±2%) The hard limit on the final rate. Bounds extreme ones.

    Both are read live, never hardcoded — Bybit exposes fundingCap on its ticker and Binance overlays it via fundingInfo. core §4 Profiles §1

    Funding is a payment, not a stream

    This is where a plausible-looking estimate goes wrong. Funding is paid at a timestamp. It does not accrue by the second. You capture a settlement only if you are holding across it — so expected capture has to enumerate the actual settlement timestamps inside the planned hold and sum them. Multiplying a per-hour rate by hours held is not an approximation of that; it is a different number. tutorial §1.7 core C2

    Worse, the interval itself is live state. Since 2025-10-30 Bybit auto-switches an alt between 8h and 1h when funding pins the cap, and reverts without notice. A position opened under one interval can be settling on another an hour later, so the monitor has to detect the flip rather than trust the interval it saw at entry. tutorial §1.7 core C8

    Mark, index, last

    Three prices exist for the same instrument at the same moment, and using the wrong one is a silent, expensive mistake.

    Last The last trade on this venue. Noisy, and manipulable by anyone willing to print one bad trade.
    Index A basket of spot prices across several venues. The "true" reference, hard to move.
    Mark A smoothed price derived from index plus basis. This is the one that drives liquidation and funding.

    Your live funding estimate is anchored to mark, not last. tutorial §1.6

    Why this shows up in the architecture

    If mark drives liquidation, then your distance to liquidation moves whenever mark moves — not only when something happens to your account. An engine that recomputes liquidation distance on position and margin events alone will sit quietly while the market walks toward the price that closes it out. tutorial §6.2

    So liquidation distance is recomputed on every fresh mark update, and a mark feed that goes stale is itself an emergency: it blocks new openings and escalates, failing over to the venue's REST mark surface, and if both stay blind past a budget a pre-authorized de-lever or close fires. core §7.1 core §6

    Liquidation, margin, and ADL

    The cash-and-carry position is delta-neutral: long spot, short perp, equal size. Neutral does not mean safe. The perp leg is leveraged and can be liquidated on a sharp rally even while the spot leg gains exactly as much — because the two legs live in different wallets and the liquidation engine only looks at one of them.

    Rescue margin is an action, not a buffer

    The intuitive fix — keep spare cash in the account — does not work under an isolated-margin posture. The isolated liquidation engine cannot see free wallet balance. Margin sitting nearby is margin that does not exist as far as the engine is concerned. core §7.1

    So the rescue is an active add-margin API call that moves margin into the position, from a float staged at the venue in advance. It has to be pre-staged, because reactive spot-to-futures transfers are unavailable exactly during the stress that needs them, and it carries a reserved rate-limit budget so it is not queued behind routine traffic. core §7.1 tutorial §6.2

    And if the top-up is too slow

    The fallback is PackageDelever — a perp-short-first reduction of both legs, keeping the package neutral as it shrinks. It is deliberately not the ordinary exposure-reduction path: the package is still hedged, so reducing a single leg would create a naked leg in the middle of the rally that triggered the emergency. core §7.1 core §5.3

    ADL — the hedge failing while you watch

    Auto-deleveraging is not a footnote. In October 2025 exchanges force-closed profitable short legs, which left desks naked long into a still-falling market. The hedge was removed by the venue, at the worst moment, without asking. core P3 tutorial §6.2

    Cash-and-carry has one narrow but real advantage here: the spot leg is not ADL'd or seized under the posture this project pins, so the whole position is never lost at once. But a force-closed short still leaves directional exposure that has to be detected from a private ADL event and reduced immediately. core §4.1

    There is a detail here that the first draft of the spec got wrong, and it is a good illustration of how specific this domain gets: to re-hedge a perp that was ADL'd to zero, you must place an ordinary short — not a reduce-only one. Reduce-only cannot open a position, so the venue would reject it. And to flatten a naked spot long you place a plain sell, because reduceOnly does not exist for spot on either venue. tutorial §6.2 core §5.3

    Binance and Bybit

    Two venues, chosen for the MVP, sitting behind one adapter contract so their differences stay contained in one place. The differences are not cosmetic — here is the flavour of what the adapter has to hide. core §4 tutorial §4.2

    A feed that dies without telling you

    Binance decommissioned its legacy /ws/ path on 2026-04-23. Funding now routes to /market, the book to /public, user data to /private. A client that was not migrated does not error — it connects, stays connected, and silently receives nothing for funding. That is the worst failure shape there is: healthy-looking and wrong. tutorial §4.2 Profiles §1

    Fields that only exist on new clients

    Bybit added fundingIntervalHour and fundingCap to its ticker (WebSocket 2025-10-16, REST 2025-10-23). A client pinned older than October 2025 will not parse them — and those are exactly the fields the interval-flip detection depends on. tutorial §4.2 Profiles §1

    The same intent, two incompatible expressions

    Closing a position is not one API call. Binance in Hedge Mode rejects reduceOnly with error −2022, while one-way mode uses it; Bybit keys off positionIdx instead. This is why position mode is pinned and verified rather than discovered at the moment you need to close. tutorial §4.2 core §4.1

    Rate limits, and the one you cannot see coming

    Binance publishes usage in an X-MBX-USED-WEIGHT header, escalating 429 then a 418 IP ban. Bybit has headers too — and an unheadered IP gateway ban that header-watching alone will never catch. You find that one by hitting it. tutorial §4.2 Profiles §1

    The asymmetry that shapes the whole safety design

    Bybit can attach a take-profit/stop-loss to an entry order atomically. Binance USDⓈ-M cannot — its conditional orders are separate placements, which means there is a window where the position exists and its protection does not. Profiles §1

    That single difference is why an independent off-host actuator is mandatory on Binance rather than a nice-to-have. Most of Part 4 exists because of it. core §7.4

    Who is on the other side

    Funding is not yield the exchange pays you. It is a transfer between traders, which the exchange orchestrates while taking no side. When you collect positive funding on a short perp, the money comes from crowded longs — leveraged traders paying to keep a position the market has made expensive to hold. tutorial §1.2

    That is worth sitting with, because it tells you what the edge is and is not. It is not mispricing. It is a fee for providing the other side of a crowded trade, and it persists only as long as the crowd does.

    You are not the only one who noticed

    The cash-and-carry level on majors is a real, persistent, positive number most of the time — and it is industrialised. Ethena runs this trade at scale as a product. Better-capitalized desks with better fee tiers are in the same position you are trying to open. tutorial §2.1

    The honest conclusion the spec draws from that, and does not soften: the edge is thin and commoditized, so the durable value is operational excellence in the plumbing — never losing a leg, never mis-reconciling, never being liquidated by a margin-tier transition you did not model. The money is in not bleeding the edge back through bugs. tutorial Closing thoughts core §2.1

    The other counterparty is the exchange itself

    For the MVP the assets sit on-exchange, deliberately, at small size, in isolated per-venue subaccounts with hard collateral caps of roughly 20–30% of the bankroll per venue — so one venue freezing cannot sink the operation. core §7.2 tutorial §6.3

    Off-exchange settlement is the documented scale-up path, not a skipped step — with two corrections worth knowing now. There is no single rail spanning Binance and Bybit (Binance reaches Ceffu MirrorX, Bybit reaches Copper and Fireblocks), so crossing pools needs two custodians. And off-exchange settlement protects custody, not margin: October 2025 liquidated those users too. tutorial §5.2 core S1

    One more correction, because it is widely misremembered: the October 2025 USDe low of roughly $0.65 was a local-venue dislocation, not a protocol depeg — Ethena processed over $2B of redemptions with zero downtime. Do not over-fit to a depeg that did not happen; do keep margin in USDT/USDC so a collateral wobble cannot cascade. tutorial §6.3 core S1
